# Critique of `benchmar_assign_taxo` — aims and code

Written 2026-09-22 at the developer's request, as an external researcher's
review. Two axes, as asked: **(1) the conceptual side** — are the aims well
defined and interesting? — and **(2) the code side**, read as the code's
*side and size*: how much code there is, where it concentrates, whether the
size is justified and what it costs to maintain. Nothing in the project was
changed; this file is the only output.

Read before writing: `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, `ROADMAP.md`,
`docs/objectives_design.md`, `docs/hleap_2021_metrics.md`,
`docs/experimental_design.md`, `docs/reference_databases.md`,
`proposals_for_dbpq.md`, `critique_fable/` (the 2026-09-10 structural review —
I do not repeat its findings unless they bear on size or on the science), and
the code: `config.R`, `make_databases.R`, `make.R`, `R/*.R` (all 14 files),
`pipelines/*.R` (4), `analysis/00_setup.R`, `analysis/01_load_and_clean.qmd`,
the other chapters, `tests/` (12 files).

Tone is deliberately harsh where it matters: the project's own documentation
culture is strong enough to survive it. Everything positive said below is
meant; everything negative comes with a fix.

---

# Part 1 — Conceptual critique

## 1.0 The aims, as I can reconstruct them

> "Which method × database (including database modifications) × parameter
> combinations give the best trade-off between assignment quality, compute
> time and compute power?" (`docs/experimental_design.md`, `README.md`)

decomposed into Q0–Q5 (trade-off, parameters, databases, consensus, ITSx,
ASV vs OTU), D1a–D1d (cross-validation, in silico, biological datasets,
second mock), M1 (performance metrics) and M2 (cost and CO₂): 11 objectives,
27 numbered decisions in `objectives_design.md` §4 plus 27 in
`docs/hleap_2021_metrics.md` §5, on 2 mock communities.

## 1.1 Are the aims well defined? Mostly yes — with four gaps

**Strengths first, briefly.** The scope sentence ("taxonomic assignment of
Fungi, and nothing else") is exactly right and has been enforced (miaSim
dropped, biological data reduced to NA rates). The metric definitions were
settled against a primary source (Hleap's paper *and* his code), which is
better than 90 % of benchmark papers manage. The per-unit truth built from
Sanger sequences is the correct answer to the set-membership problem, and the
decision log with dates and verbatim answers makes the design auditable. This
is above-average experimental hygiene. Now the gaps.

**C1. The central question is a benchmark question, not a hypothesis.**
"Which combination gives the best trade-off?" is a shopping question for
practitioners; it is answerable by a table and does not need a paper. As
written there is no falsifiable claim anywhere in the design — no "we predict
that…". Yet the design already contains at least four predictions that could
carry the manuscript:

- **P1a.** Restricting a reference to Fungi makes non-target sequences
  *more* likely to be called Fungi (`ext_fungi` ↑), and adding back a few
  percent of non-fungal representatives (`fungi + rep`) removes most of that
  bias at little quality cost. This is a real, novel, testable claim.
- **P1b.** k-mer methods degrade with the *taxonomic breadth* of the
  reference, not its size (already measured ad hoc: dada2 gives 0 % genus on
  full `EUK_ITS_v2.1`, 95 % on `_Fungi`, and the bootstrap — not the
  assignment — is what collapses, ROADMAP "Databases building"). A designed
  test of this would be a result on its own.
- **P1c.** Parameter sensitivity differs by method family: abstaining methods
  (blastn) trade FP for NA along `min_id`, k-mer methods do not.
- **P1d.** Reference trimming to the amplicon is a no-op when the primer sites
  lie in flanks the reference does not carry (`docs/experimental_design.md`
  §5.5) — a general caution about "region-specific references" that outlives
  this paper.

**Proposition (P1).** Write the Introduction around 3–4 falsifiable
predictions; the grid is already able to test all of them at zero extra
compute. Move the central trade-off question to the practical conclusion
("given the results, here is the recipe"), which is where it belongs.

**C2. Objective sprawl, no primary endpoint.** Eleven objectives, three units
(ASV, OTU, OTU_derived), three inputs, nine databases, 21 parameter rows,
~20 metrics × 7 ranks, two mocks: the design generates on the order of
10⁵ numbers before a single figure. Nothing designates a *primary* outcome,
so every reader (and every reviewer) will fear that whichever comparison turns
out nice becomes the headline. The risk is not statistical here, it is
rhetorical: an omnibus with 27 decisions cannot be reviewed in a reasonable
time.

**Proposition (P2).** Pre-specify one primary endpoint — I suggest **F1 and
PPV at Genus and Species on the two mocks** — and label everything else
secondary or exploratory *in the manuscript*. Consider dropping or demoting
Q3 (consensus voting) to supplementary: see T1 below, its result is largely
mechanical.

**C3. "Best trade-off" is under-defined and, as designed, optimistically
biased.** Two problems.

1. The quality axis is F1/PPV/TPR at 7 ranks × several metrics and the cost
   axis is wall time / peak RAM / CPU / CO₂eq; the Pareto front is fine, but
   parameter rows *share* their computation's cost by construction
   (`R/assign_compute.R`), so along a sweep only quality moves and the front
   degenerates into vertical stacks. That is a fine thing to show — state it,
   or the figure looks like a bug.
2. More seriously: **every parameter row is scored on the same mock used to
   pick it.** Reporting "the best parameter set" from a sweep evaluated in
   sample is a winner's-curse estimate. Hleap's split (tune on ⅓, score on ⅔)
   exists precisely to avoid this; the decision to differ (hleap §5 point 8)
   is defensible for the *sensitivity* question but must not leak into a
   *recommendation*.

**Proposition (P3).** The two mocks give you the split for free: select the
best (method × database × parameter) on **Pauvert**, confirm it on
**Tedersoo**, and report that agreement/disagreement as a result ("agreement
between the two mocks is itself a result" — your own words in the Q1 row).
Costs nothing; removes the bias from any recommendation.

**C4. The truth table's semantics need one more sentence in the paper.**
Units without a Sanger match are kept in the confusion matrix where they can
only be FN (if the method abstains) or FP (if it answers) — Q2b, deliberately,
because the aim is a *relative* comparison. Correct, but it means each score
mixes "is the method right?" with "how often does the method speak?". With
42 % of the Tedersoo ASVs carrying no truth (105 with no Sanger hit at all),
that mixing is not a rounding error there.

**Proposition (P4).** A sensitivity run with truthless units left out of the
matrix (the machinery exists: `truth_depth` already removes tied units below
their common rank). If the ranking of Q2 survives, say so; if it flips, the
paper must carry both.

## 1.2 Are the aims interesting? Yes — but the novelty is not where the work is

The audience (Molecular Ecology Resources / Environmental DNA / Methods in
Ecology and Evolution) already has: Wang et al. 2007 (RDP), Lan et al. 2012,
Allard et al. 2015, Edgar 2018 (TAXXI), Murali et al. 2018 (IDTAXA), Bokulich
et al. 2018 (q2-feature-classifier), Kaehler et al. 2019, **Hleap et al.
2021** (eukaryotic metabarcoding assignment — the direct competitor), plus the
2025–26 wave (Graetz et al. 2025 ONT mock; Tosadori & Bosch 2026 ASV vs OTU
through six pipelines). A reviewer's first question will be "why another
benchmark?".

**C5. The genuine novelties are four and none of them is the headline
question.**

1. **Database curation as an experimental factor** (all → Fungi → Fungi+rep),
   with `ext_fungi` / `ext_correct` measuring the over-assignment that
   Fungi-only references induce. I know of no published benchmark that tests
   this, and everyone does it. *This is the paper.*
2. **Negative controls as scored units** (shuffled + external, in the
   confusion matrix per rank). Hleap shuffles; nobody scores outgroups
   per rank.
3. **Parameter sensitivity as the object** instead of tuned best scores
   (21 rows derived at zero compute cost — an elegant design).
4. **Cost and CO₂ as a first-class axis** (timely, rarely done seriously).

Everything else is replication: Q5 (ASV vs OTU) is Tosadori & Bosch 2026 with
fewer pipelines; Q3 (consensus across assignments) is well covered elsewhere
and — see T1 — its outcome here is largely determined by the column set;
Q4 (ITSx) is useful engineering but a secondary note; D1b/D1c are support
material.

**Proposition (P5).** Restructure the paper around items 1–3 (drop or demote
Q3, fold Q4 into the methods, present Q5 as confirmation of Tosadori & Bosch
on a mock with per-sequence truth). And **promote the two findings currently
buried in ROADMAP prose to Results**: the dada2 bootstrap collapse under
taxonomic breadth (§ "Databases building") and the `_cut` no-op
(`experimental_design.md` §5.5). Both are more interesting than most of Q1.

## 1.3 Threats to the conclusions (the reviewer in me)

**T1. Pseudo-replication in the database axis, and a mechanical Q3.**
The "nine databases" are not nine treatments: `Unite_all` ⊂ `Unite_s_all`
(without singletons is a subset of with), and `_Fungi` ⊂ `_Fungi+rep` ⊂
`full`. There are **two independent providers** (UNITE, EUKARYOME) crossed
with a filtering ladder. Any general statement about "databases" rests on
n = 2. Worse for Q3: its 36 voting columns include the nested UNITE pair and
two alignment methods (lca, blastn) reading the same reference — the vote
partly counts the same evidence twice, and the 2026-09-22 fix (default
settings only) fixed the *weighting* but not the *redundancy*. A consensus
over correlated columns is expected to beat any single column by construction.

**Proposition (P6).** Analyse Q2 as paired within-source contrasts (already
proposed in `objectives_design.md` Q2 — good) and write "UNITE vs EUKARYOME",
not "databases", in the conclusions. For Q3, either vote over one column per
*source × method family* (e.g. 2 sources × 3 families), or report the
consensus gain against its *effective* number of independent voters.

**T2. The benchmark is closed-book.** Every mock species is, by construction,
well represented in UNITE/EUKARYOME (134–163 of 181 Pauvert species found by
exact name match). So the mocks measure **identification of known taxa**, not
taxonomic discovery — the situation where practitioners actually need help
(novel/environmental lineages) is untouched. The standard (non-leaked) CV
variant is the only novelty test, and it is contested (T7).

**Proposition (P7).** Add one novelty axis, cheaply: leave *one mock species
out of the reference* (all its records removed) and measure what the methods
say about its ASVs — over-assignment to a neighbour is exactly the failure the
controls detect, now on real sequences. One extra assignment run per database
at most, or zero if done as a scoring variant of the existing CV.

**T3. Design constants leak into the headline metrics.** `prop_fake = 0.5`
and 100 `external_` records make roughly half of the scored units synthetic.
Your own `docs/hleap_2021_metrics.md` §1.6 states that F1, PPV and MCC then
depend on `prop_fake` and on the control count — arbitrary constants of the
design. Add the mono-nucleotide shuffle (easier to reject than Hleap's
di-nucleotide one, so TNR is inflated for k-mer methods) and the absolute
values of the headline metrics are properties of the harness as much as of the
methods. Relative comparisons survive; absolute numbers should not be
published without this caveat.

**Proposition (P8).** Headline F1/PPV/TPR **on real units only**; carry the
controls through their own rates (`NA_fake`, `ext_fungi`, `ext_correct`,
`ctrl_assigned`), which is what they measure; or normalise TP/FP/FN by the
number of real units and TN by the number of controls and never mix the two
denominators in one metric. (The per-unit scoring already separates the cells;
it is a reporting choice, not a rewrite.)

**T4. Two mocks, one lab, and region confounded with community.** Pauvert
(ITS1, 196 ASVs) and Tedersoo (full ITS, 238 ASVs) differ in marker region,
primer pair, sequencing run and community at once, so any Pauvert/Tedersoo
disagreement — announced as "itself a result" in Q1 — is uninterpretable
between these causes. And the "TDR/TAR agreement between mocks" claim rests
on n = 2 datasets.

**Proposition (P9).** State the confound explicitly and restrict the claim to
"two mock communities differing in ITS region and protocol"; do not attribute
disagreement to the region without the third mock (SRR30413326, D1d) or the
in silico set, which can separate the two (same community, two regions is
possible in silico at zero wet-lab cost — a good use of D1b).

**T5. Synonym-tolerant scoring interacts with the very effect Q2 studies.**
"Either the submitted or the GBIF accepted name counts as correct" is kind,
but UNITE and EUKARYOME differ *in nomenclature* as much as in content: a
method may get a TP thanks to the tolerance on one database and not on the
other. The choice can therefore mask or manufacture Q2 differences.

**Proposition (P10).** One sensitivity column: strict submitted-name-only
scoring. The truth table already carries `Genus_accepted` / `Species_accepted`,
so this is a one-line variant of `tc_metrics_unit()`'s comparison.

**T6. No uncertainty quantification on the mock metrics.** The same ~150–200
units are scored under every one of the 504 × 7 cells: the data are perfectly
paired and the units are few. Today Q2 will show point estimates and
eye-judged differences between, say, F1 = 0.78 and 0.76 computed on 149
units. With 504 rows × 20 metrics × 7 ranks, some "effects" are noise by
construction.

**Proposition (P11).** Bootstrap the units (10⁴ resamples of the ~150 scored
ASVs, recompute the paired Δ between two databases on each) for CIs on the
*paired differences* — cheap (the confusion cells are per-unit; resampling is
counts arithmetic) and it converts every Q2 claim into a supported one.
McNemar on the discordant units is the natural per-pair companion.

**T7. Cross-validation measures database-internal consistency, not accuracy.**
The CV truth is the database's own label, so it rewards the method that best
reproduces that database — circular for the "which database is best" question
and biased towards the database used as training. Add: (i) CV queries are
fungal-only (OD 20), so CV **cannot** test the non-fungal rejection that is
the whole point of `fungi + rep`; (ii) unstratified random folds give a
database-dependent share (6–34 %, your own measurement in S8.4) of queries
whose genus is absent from the training part, so the CV database comparison
confounds reference composition with novelty rate; (iii) the accepted
flattery of leave-one-out-style splits is exactly Edgar 2018's critique.

**Proposition (P12).** Give D1a a single explicit estimand in the Methods
("consistency of each method with each database's own labels at fixed
reference size"), add the negative controls to the folds (your own TODO in
`R/cross_val.R` — this finally gives a CV-side TNR), and either stratify the
draw by genus or report the per-database novelty rate beside every CV figure.

**T8. The CO₂ axis rests on one machine and one tool's assumptions.**
`greenAlgoR::ga_footprint()` turns wall time and core counts into CO₂eq given
a grid carbon intensity and a hardware model; M2's own measurement layer is
known to be partly invalid (autometric logs mixing runs, `experimental_design`
§3) and CO₂ estimates were computed with the wrong thread counts before S6.8.
This axis is worth having, but it is the least defensible number in the paper.

**Proposition (P13).** Keep M2 secondary: report wall time and peak RAM as
measured (with a clean rerun on the 96-computation grid) and CO₂eq only with a
sensitivity note (±2× on the carbon intensity changes nothing to the ranking —
if true, say exactly that).

**T9. D1c (biological datasets) cannot validate anything.** NA rates on real
communities with no truth can show that the mocks are not wildly unrepresentative
in their NA behaviour; they cannot support accuracy claims. Fine as
"ecological realism" — but the section should say so in one sentence, or a
reviewer will treat it as anecdote and ask for real validation data.

## 1.4 Conceptual propositions, by value for the paper

| # | Proposition | Cost | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Reframe around 3–4 falsifiable predictions | writing | makes it a paper, not a table |
| P5 | Promote the bootstrap-collapse and `_cut` no-op findings; demote Q3/Q5 | writing | the two most citable results |
| P3 | Select on Pauvert, confirm on Tedersoo for any recommendation | 0 compute | removes winner's-curse bias |
| P11 | Bootstrap CIs on paired differences | hours | Q2 claims become supported |
| P8 | Headline metrics on real units; controls via their own rates | reporting | metrics stop depending on harness constants |
| P6 | Q2 as within-source contrasts; Q3 on independent voters | analysis | kills pseudo-replication |
| P4, P10 | Two sensitivity analyses (truthless units; strict names) | 0–1 run | closes two loopholes |
| P7 | One-species-out novelty test | ≤ 1 grid run | turns a closed-book benchmark into an open one |
| P12 | CV estimand + controls in folds + novelty rate per database | modest | saves D1a from circularity |
| P9, P13 | Confound and CO₂ caveats | writing | pre-empts predictable objections |

("Code size" read as the code's size *and* the code side generally; the

**Verdict on size: not bloated.** ~7 500 lines of code for 96 computations ×
several datasets, 504 assignment rows, per-unit truth, a cost layer and a
Quarto analysis is a defensible ratio, and 2 000 test lines is unusual in a
benchmark project (a virtue). The problem is not the *quantity* but its
**distribution**: one function holds a third of the risk, roughly 400 lines are
dead or duplicated (below), and the documentation mass (4 588 lines, six
documents describing the same design) is now a liability of its own.

## 2.2 Where the code is too big

**S1. `R/cross_val.R`: 625 lines in a single function, much of it duplicated.**
`cross_val()` is 88 % of its file and is simultaneously: a query sampler, a
cutadapt caller, a fold engine, four method adapters and a metric computer.
Concretely:

- the metric block is written **twice** (vector `min_bootstrap` branch lines
  363–524, scalar branch lines 525–648: ~120 lines each, diverging subtly —
  e.g. the `tib_by_tax_level` shapes differ, 6 columns vs 5);
- the `compute_by_tax_level` sub-block is duplicated in **both** branches
  (the ~45-line per-rank loop at lines 422–464 and 571–598, plus its
  accumulator plumbing in each), and accumulates with `rbind` inside a loop
  over every rank × value — the quadratic idiom in R;
- `res_assign_NA_classif` and `res_assign_NA_database` are computed (lines
  411–412, 560–561) and then **dropped**: `final_res` never returns them,
  although `docs/objectives_design.md` §1.6 and the D1a metrics row promise
  `prop_NA` split into `NA_classif` / `NA_database`, and `tests/test_cv_to_tidy.R`
  already feeds a `NA_classif` metric. The design document describes
  behaviour the code does not have — exactly the drift class this project
  otherwise guards against;
- a dead `dada2_2steps` branch (lines 314–325, `stop()` at the top), a
  French TODO block (lines 21–32) and roxygen stubs (`#' @param ref_fasta`
  empty, `#TODO complete documentation`, empty `@returns` / `@examples`) in a
  project whose `CLAUDE.md` says "it is not an R package";
- the invariant check at lines 472–477 and 600–605 compares a **sum of
  floating-point proportions** to an integer with `!=`. It passes today by
  luck of the roundings; `abs(x - n) > 1e-8` or a per-row check would be the
  same message without the risk.

**Proposition (P14).** Extract one `cv_score(assignment, truth, min_bootstrap)`
returning a long tibble (unit × rank × metric), call it from both branches and
delete ~230 lines. `compute_by_tax_level` either becomes a separate summariser
over that long tibble (then it is 15 lines and works for every method) or is
deleted — today it is exercised only by `R/examples_cross_val.R`, which
nothing sources. Return `NA_classif` / `NA_database` or amend the design doc.
Target: `cross_val()` under 300 lines.

**S2. `make_databases.R` (892 lines): dead functions and shell-string FASTA
surgery.** `derive_clustered()` (lines 381–405) and `derive_no_parens()`
(lines 283–293) are never called (the file's own header says so) — yet both
appear in `proposals_for_dbpq.md` as upstream candidates: **do not upstream
dead code**. And the header rewrites are `sed` / `awk` programs embedded as
strings (`general_header_rules`, `derive_no_pattern`, `derive_mini`) with
manual shell quoting. This is the fragile layer the project has already been
bitten by once: `docs/hleap_2021_metrics.md` §6 records a `awk` under a French
locale parsing `99.5` as `99` and undercounting every threshold. String-typed
FASTA surgery has no type checking and no unit-testable semantics.

**Proposition (P15).** Delete the two dead functions (and remove them from
`proposals_for_dbpq.md`). Replace the `sed`/`awk` header rewrites with the
Biostrings + stringr code the same file already uses for `derive_fake_ref()` /
`derive_fungi_rep()`; keep the shell only where the tool demands it (cutadapt,
vsearch, 7z). −120 dead lines, and the parsing bugs become testable R.

**S3. Metadata encoded in target names, re-parsed by regex everywhere.**
`full_name_for()` glues method, database and parameters into a string with
`"..."` separators (`blastn__EUK_ITS_v2.1___0.5...rel_majority...100...95`);
then `enrich_metrics()` (`analysis/00_setup.R:154-204`) recovers the parts
with `gsub` chains and lookbehind regexes, `assignment_db()`
(`R/score_assignments.R:26`) re-derives the database, and the `na-prop` chunk
of chapter 01 (lines 486–513) writes a **third** copy of the same parsing with
its own `grepl` ladder. Two consequences: (i) three places must agree on a
name grammar that no test pins as a grammar (only as byte strings,
`tests/test_values_map.R`); (ii) a database name containing `___` or `...`
silently corrupts every downstream grouping. The same smell appears as
**two definitions of "default settings"** — `filter_default_settings()`
(`00_setup.R:139-147`) and `default_names_for()` (chapter 01, lines 177–186) —
which the ROADMAP itself flags for merging (0.4d).

**Proposition (P16).** Keep the target names (they are pinned and fine as
*identifiers*) but stop parsing them: `values_map` already carries `method`,
`db`, `min_bootstrap`, `lca_cutoff`, `vote_algorithm`, `min_id` as columns.
Carry that one row through the scoring result (a `left_join` on `full_name`)
and delete `enrich_metrics()`'s regex half, `assignment_db()` and the
`na-prop` ladder (~90 lines gone). Merge the two default-settings definitions
into one function returning both the filter and the names.

**S4. `analysis/01_load_and_clean.qmd` (610 lines) is four programs in one
document**: taxonomy cleaning + `Gen_sp` construction, consensus construction
(9 configurations × 7 ranks), scoring orchestration over 3 units, the M1.5
abundance sweep, invariant checks, and ~130 lines of exploratory figures.
The first four are reusable library code trapped in a qmd (the project already
knows this: `R/score_assignments.R` was extracted for exactly this reason).

**Proposition (P17).** Move `clean_taxo()`, `add_gen_sp()` and
`add_consensus_columns()` to `R/` next to `score_assignments.R` (they are
tested indirectly today, and not at all in fact), keep the chapter to
orchestration + checks. The exploratory views belong in `sandbox/` — a
chapter that must render in CI should not also draw ad hoc plots.

**S5. Dead and stray files at the top level.** `R/examples_cross_val.R`
(134 lines, sourced by nothing — the 2026-09-10 review already proposed
moving it), `Rplots.pdf` (stray device output), `run_assign_taxo.log`,
`v2.31.0.tar.gz` (vsearch source tarball, untracked since S0.5 but still
occupying the root). ~350 lines + clutter for zero readers.

**Proposition (P18).** Move `examples_cross_val.R` to `Archives/` or delete;
gitignore or delete the three stray files.

## 2.3 Size that is not code: the documentation mass

**S6. Six documents describe the same design** (`README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`,
`CONTEXT.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, `docs/objectives_design.md`,
`docs/experimental_design.md`, plus `docs/hleap_2021_metrics.md` and
`critique_fable/`): 4 588 lines against 7 537 of code. The provenance culture
is genuinely valuable — the `hleap_2021_metrics.md` analysis is the kind of
primary-source work that makes the science defensible — but the *same* design
facts (the grid, the computation counts, the metric set) are stated in at
least four places, and "documentation drift" is already a named bug class in
this project (S4.2, and `CLAUDE.md` carries a standing "counts outdated"
warning about `experimental_design.md`). The drift will win.

**S7. `ROADMAP.md` is a changelog wearing a todo's clothes.** 24 of its lines
exceed 1 200 characters (several exceed 2 000 — the truncation is visible in
any viewer), carrying dated narratives, measurements and superseded decisions
inside checklist items. As a todo it is unusable (what is actually left is
"section 0, items 0.11a–c" plus a handful of unticked items); as a log it is
unsearchable.

**Proposition (P19).** One source of truth for design facts
(`docs/objectives_design.md`), with README/CLAUDE/CONTEXT pointing at it and
stating only what is *local* to their concern. Split `ROADMAP.md` into a
one-line-per-item todo and `CHANGELOG.md` (or `journal/`) for the narratives;
the done log is already the germ of it. Rough effect: the docs shrink by half
and, more importantly, a fact has one place to be corrected.

## 2.4 Smaller code observations (correctness and robustness)

- **R1.** `R/values_map.R:215-224`: `db_path_for(rep("sintax", length(db)), …)`
  — callers must remember to fake a vector to satisfy `ifelse()` inside
  `db_path_for()` / `ref_file_target_for()`. Either vectorise properly
  (`ifelse(length(method) == 1, rep(method, length(db)), method)`) or drop
  `ifelse` for `dplyr::case_when`; the comment explaining the trick is longer
  than the fix.
- **R2.** `R/assign_compute.R:74-76`: `derive_assignment()` silently returns
  `physeq` unchanged when `computed` is NULL. The only downstream catch is the
  empty-assignment guard in `combine_taxo_assignments()`; a `warning()` at the

## 2.5 Verdict on the code side

The code is, on the whole, **better than the average research pipeline**:
idempotent database derivation, file-targeted invalidation, smoke-test
isolation (`*_mini` stores), per-target cost logging, and a test suite that
found real bugs (its own record shows it). The size is justified *as a
whole*; what is not justified is (i) the 625-line `cross_val()` carrying
duplicated and dead branches plus metrics the design document promises and the
code drops (−230 lines available), (ii) ~250 lines of dead code and un-sourced
examples, (iii) three competing parsers of a name-encoded metadata grammar
(−90 lines and a whole class of silent corruption available), and (iv) 4 588
lines of documentation with six overlapping truths — the largest maintenance
liability in the repository, and the one least likely to be fixed by more
documentation.

Trimmed as proposed, the project lands at roughly 7 000 lines of code with one
place per fact — and, more importantly, `cross_val()` and the name grammar
would finally be testable, which matters because D1a is the objective with the
weakest science (T7) and the least code coverage (R6): the two problems sit in
the same file.

(2026) roots this project — composition-based algorithms (all of ours but

What this settles and what it changes in the critique above:

- **Method set (was the "method gap"):** closed as a *deliberate zoom*, not a
  gap — provided the paper says so and cites Orsholm for the family question.
  One nuance to keep honest in the writing: LCA (vsearch alignment + LCA of
  top hits) is search-based like blastn, so the zoom is 2 composition-based
  (dada2, sintax) × 2 similarity-search (lca, blastn) methods, with blastn as
  the abstaining contrast. My P7 (one-species-out) and P11 (bootstrap CIs)
  stand unchanged.
- **Q3 (voting) is a primary axis — P5's "demote Q3" is withdrawn.** Q2 and
  Q3 are the paper's two complexity layers, per the framing above.
- **Consequence for my own T1:** with Q3 as a headline claim, its fix
  (**P6**) is no longer optional but **required**. A vote over columns that
  include the nested UNITE pair and two search-based methods cannot support a
  claim about voting: the gain would partly measure counted-twice evidence.
  The paper's Q3 hypothesis should be the one the framing implies — *voting
  helps through error independence, so it gains from method-family and
  provider diversity, not from column count* — and it is testable at zero
  compute on the existing columns: `resolve_taxo_conflict()` on subsets
  (composition-only vs mixed families; one provider vs both; with and without
  the nested UNITE pair). This becomes **P21** and should be added to ROADMAP
  section 0.11 with the Q3 figures.
- **Q5** (was demoted with Q3): unchanged — replication of Tosadori & Bosch
  2026 belongs in supplementary or as a confirmation.

  assigners, comparpq for `resolve_taxo_conflict()` and the scoring) — one
